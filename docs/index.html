The question

How predictable is what we listen to?

If a one-line rule can guess your next artist, how much of your listening is choice? We tested simple rules on a year of real listening.

What we did
  • Listeners
  • RulesFour one-line guessing rules, no learning: same artist as last time, your most-played artist, the artist that usually follows this one, and what you usually play at this hour. Rules ↓
  • ScoringEach rule learns from a listener's first 80% of the year, then guesses the artist of every listen in the last 20%. A guess is right if it names any credited artist. Method ↓

        Is a smarter model much better?

        Three numbers per typical listener: the best single rule, a learned mix of all four rules (weights fitted per listener), and the ceiling: listens where any rule's first guess was right, which no mix of these rules can beat.

        Not obvious from the numbers
          Loops and breakouts

          A loop day: one track is at least 30% of the day's plays and at least 5 plays. A breakout month: first-time artists well above that listener's own normal (more than 2 SD).

          Not obvious from the numbers
            Time of day

            ListenBrainz has no timezone or location. The blue line treats everyone as UTC. The orange line shifts each listener so their quietest (sleeping) hours fall at 5 a.m. local, an estimate from their own daily rhythm.

              Method

              How it was measured

                The four rules

                • Same as last: guess the artist of the previous listen (top-5: the five most recent distinct artists).
                • Most played: guess the listener's most-played artist in their training period.
                • Usually next: guess the artist that most often followed the current one in training, else most played.
                • This hour: guess the most-played artist in this hour of the week (168 buckets) in training, else most played.

                Who counts as an artist

                  Read with care

                  Limits

                  • What played, not why. Your own picks, autoplay and algorithmic playlists look the same in this data. High predictability can mean habit or a recommender doing the choosing.
                  • No skips. ListenBrainz records a listen after about 30 seconds or half the track; skips before that are invisible.
                  • A self-selected crowd. People who set up ListenBrainz are music-nerdy and heavy listeners (3,000+ listens a year was required).
                  • Mixed services. Spotify, Last.fm imports, local players and more, all mixed together.
                  • No timezones. All times are UTC; the local-time view is an estimate.
                  • One year. Trends within the year are weak evidence (seasonality, one window).
                  • Artist splitting is imperfect. “You & I” becomes two artists; parenthesised credits are not split. Well under 0.1% of listens.
